# The Ask Najj episode template

**Version 1.0 — 21 Sep 2026.** The fixed template. Every episode is built from this; only the question, the building and the
numbers change. Companion to `ASK_NAJJ_VIDEO_BIBLE.md`, which holds the voice, the harness, the filing and the corrections log.

Built from: two research passes (platform mechanics and retention; vertical film craft), Naj's workflow brief of 21 Sep, Kendall's
background rule, and the measured behaviour of the five episodes already shot. Evidence is graded in place —
**[STRONG]** large-N or primary, **[MODERATE]** credible but vendor-owned, **[CRAFT]** film-craft consensus, **[JUDGEMENT]** mine.
Where the honest answer was "no public data exists", it says so rather than inventing a number.

## 2. The frame

Everything that matters lives inside one box. This is the single most common production failure in the format, and our current
episodes fail it.

```
1080 × 1920
┌──────────────────────────────┐  y 0
│   dead — platform UI         │
├──────────────────────────────┤  y 270   ← top of the safe box (Meta 14%)
│  TITLE BAND    y 270–560     │          question · act label · building name · the disclosure line
│                              │
│  HERO BAND     y 560–1010    │          the 3D scene. Always the largest, always the priority
│                              │
│  DATA BAND     y 1010–1248   │          the panel. Arrives, does its job, leaves
├──────────────────────────────┤  y 1248  ← bottom of the safe box (Meta 35%)
│  PRESENTER + CAPTIONS        │          Naj's head and shoulders · burned-in captions
│  dead — caption bar, CTA,    │          NOTHING READABLE BELOW y 1248
│  action rail (right 180 px)  │
└──────────────────────────────┘  y 1920
```

- **Usable box: x 65–900, y 270–1248 — 835 × 978 px.** Meta publishes 14% top / 35% bottom / 6% sides as a recommendation;
  the right edge is widened to 180 px for TikTok's action rail. **[MODERATE — Meta figure well corroborated, TikTok inferred;
  verify once by screenshotting a real post on a real device.]**
- **The app screen recording must be legible inside 835 × 978**, not 1080 × 1920. That means filming the app's phone layout and
  then scaling the meaningful region into the box — not filming full-bleed and hoping.
- **Naj's head must sit above y 1600 and must never be readable content.** Our current episodes put the avatar in the bottom
  third, which is where the caption bar and CTA live. Head at **≤ 20% of frame height**; she is the narrator, not the subject.
- **No circle, no bubble, no border, no drop shadow on the avatar.** That is the strongest single "AI corporate explainer"
  signal in the medium. Matte her against the scene with a soft bottom fade. **[CRAFT]**
- **No letterbox bars, ever.** Instagram names *bordered* reels among five things it makes less visible, alongside low-resolution,
  watermarked, muted and majority-text. Four of those five are export-settings failures. **[STRONG — primary, Instagram]**

## 3. The 45-second template

**The fixed shape.** 11 shots. Average shot 4.1 s. Turn at 25 s (55%). An **event every 2–3 s**, a **cut every 4–6 s**.

> **The reconciliation that matters:** a visual *event* is not a *cut*. A camera move starting, a card opening, a panel
> scrolling, a colour changing, a label landing — all are events. Feature-film average shot length is ~2.5 s today, down from
> ~12 s in 1930 **[STRONG — Salt/Cinemetrics, 15,000+ films]**; short-form vendor data claims 2.5–4 s cadences **[WEAK]**.
> Target events at 2–3 s and cuts at 4–6 s and you keep cinematic shot lengths without losing retention. **[JUDGEMENT]**

## 4. The signature shot — the occlusion reveal

**Every episode's payoff is the same move, and it becomes the format's fingerprint.**

Start the camera so the subject building is **hidden behind a neighbouring mass**. Arc until it emerges. That is a textbook
Hollywood reveal executed purely with geometry, it costs nothing in a digital twin, and almost nobody in property video does it.
**[CRAFT for the mechanism, [JUDGEMENT] for making it the signature]**

The three rules that make a data reveal feel authored rather than assembled **[CRAFT + JUDGEMENT]**:

1. The camera is **already moving** when the answer beat begins.
2. The number lands on a specific frame **during** the move — not before, not after.
3. The camera **eases to a complete stop** within ~1 s of the number landing, and holds still for 2 s or more.

Motion → stillness is the visual full stop. **The payoff is the only shot in the episode allowed to come to a complete stop.**

And the convergence worth noticing: **episode 1 on the slate *is* an occlusion reveal, literally.** ONE River Point's east view is
blocked by One by Binghatti — which stands **344 m away and 87 m taller** (140 m against 227 m). Turn the camera east and the
blocker really is standing in the way. That is the format's signature shot and the episode's factual answer in the same move.

## 5. The camera — hard numbers

No published study exists on orbit speed or on how long a move holds attention; the figures below are craft-calibrated for a
phone-sized 9:16 frame. **[JUDGEMENT, on [CRAFT] foundations]**

| Parameter | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Orbit speed | **8–12°/s** | Under ~4°/s reads as stalled on a phone; over ~20°/s destroys facade legibility |
| Arc per shot | **30–60°**, never 360° | Past ~90° the silhouette repeats and says nothing |
| Start and end | **Corner-on → face-on** | The silhouette changes most in the first 45° from a corner |
| Easing | `easeInOut` to settle; `easeOut` only when cutting away mid-move | Cutting on motion hides the cut |
| Push-in | Slow: subject grows 3–5% over 3–4 s | Dawning significance. Fast (>15% in under 1 s) is shock — use once, or never |
| Altitude | Constant, unless the change *is* the point | Orbit + boom together reads as a drone showreel |
| Axes | **One dominant per shot**; a second at <20% magnitude | Multi-axis simultaneity reads as a screensaver |
| Cut points | Change azimuth **≥30°** *and* distance between two views of the same subject | The 30° rule. Less and the cut reads as a render glitch |
| Continuous move | **2.5–5 s**, then cut | Cut when the move stops producing new information |

**Banned outright:** animating field of view instead of translating the camera (the single biggest demo-versus-cinema tell);
linear interpolation (the fingerprint of an automated camera — always ease); a rolling horizon (lock the up-vector); lateral pans
in a narrow frame (they exit instantly and read as restless — prefer **boom and tilt**, the native 9:16 moves); starting a move
exactly on a cut (hold 6–10 frames of stillness first, or cut into an already-moving camera).

**Never show the clicking.** Cut on the tap, land on the opened card. Cursor travel, hover states and open animations are the
demo-video signature. Our harness currently films all three.

**Only one band moves at a time.** If the camera is orbiting, the panel is frozen. When the panel animates, the camera holds.
When a title lands, nothing else changes for 12 frames either side. This one rule does more for perceived production value than
any grade. **[JUDGEMENT]**

## 6. Text, captions and the send frame

**Hold formula:** `seconds = characters ÷ 15 + 0.5`, floor 1.2 s. A 20-character label holds 1.8 s. Netflix allows 20 characters
per second and the BBC 15; use **15**, because our viewer is parsing a 3D scene, a data panel and a voice at the same time.
**[STRONG — Netflix/BBC subtitle standards]**

- **The answer number is special:** minimum **2.5–3.5 s**, on a static or settling frame.
- **Land a label 12–20 frames after the cut, never on it** — the eye needs to find the subject first. **[CRAFT]**
- **Never label during motion.** Hold the move, land the shot, then title. Motion and reading compete directly.
- **One title card per episode**, at the turn. Two and it reads as a slideshow.
- **One type family, one weight, two sizes.** All-caps for labels of three words or fewer.

**Captions: burn them in.** 69% of consumers watch with sound off in public and captions make viewers up to **80% more likely to
finish** **[STRONG — Verizon Media/Publicis 2019, still unreplaced]**. TikTok reports a **55.7% lift in impressions** with closed
captions **[MODERATE]**. Vendor A/B across ~14,000 videos: adding captions at all is **+24% completion**, and everything after
that is small — word-by-word +11%, larger font +8%, and **branded typography −2%** **[MODERATE, leaning WEAK]**.

> So: captions in one neutral, high-legibility system face inside the bottom band. **Do not spend production time on caption
> styling.** Spend it on making more episodes that have captions at all. Keep designed typography for the data labels above.

**The send frame (shot 11).** Instagram's ranking signals, per Mosseri (Jan 2025), are **watch time, sends per reach, likes per
reach** — and sends dominate reach to non-followers. Reel shares rose **67.19% year on year** across 24.4 M posts.
**[STRONG]** A property buyer's natural act is forwarding to a spouse or partner. So the last held frame is built to be
forwarded: the number, the building, the comparison, the proof — one frame that carries the whole answer without sound.

## 7. Look and sound

**The look is made by the light, not the grade.** Lock one sun angle, one sky, one time of day for the whole series — say 16:00,
warm low sun, long shadows — as a fixed scene preset. Consistency of light across episodes builds series identity, and in a
synthetic scene it is free. **[JUDGEMENT]**

Five fixes that make a dark, UI-heavy frame feel photographed rather than screen-grabbed **[CRAFT on the science]**:

1. **Never pure black.** Lift the floor to RGB 12–18, slightly cool. A toe in the curve is what reads as photographed.
2. **Never pure white text.** 88–92%. Pure white is a screenshot signal.
3. **One saturated accent hue** — ours is the gold. Everything else desaturated. Two accents is why things look like dashboards.
4. **Light direction across the whole composite** — a gentle top-to-bottom luminance ramp and a soft vignette on the *flattened*
   frame, not per layer.
5. **Bloom on emissive UI only**, low threshold, small radius.

**Making Naj and the screen recording one image**, in order of impact:

1. **One grain pass over the flattened composite**, ~1–2% at 1080. Nothing else unifies layers as cheaply.
2. **Light wrap** — sample the scene colour at her silhouette and wrap 2–6 px onto her.
3. **One grade node on top of the stack**, not two separate grades.
4. **A contact shadow** under and behind her, so she occludes the scene rather than floating on it.
5. **Sympathetic parallax** — when the 3D camera moves, drift her 0.5–1% in the same direction. A perfectly locked foreground
   over a moving background splits the frame into two obvious layers, and it is the tell nobody diagnoses.

**Sound.** The one peer-reviewed finding that bears on this: in instructional video, music in a *pre-training* message improved
retention, but the same music **under the narration had no notable effect** **[STRONG — Journal of Educators Online]**. So music
is tonal and structural here, never informational.

- Dialogue ≈ −16 LUFS; master ≈ −14 LUFS. Music **18–22 dB below** dialogue, side-chain ducked.
- **A city ambience bed at ≈ −40 dB.** This is the difference between "a 3D model" and "a place", and almost nobody does it.
- **Silence is the strongest tool available.** Drop the bed entirely for 0.5–1.5 s before the answer number lands.
- **Land the turn on a musical event** — a drop, a new layer, a filter opening.
- Sound vocabulary: tap 40–80 ms low-mid with no high fizz; panel open = whoosh **plus settle** (the settle sells it); camera move
  = sub-200 Hz swell, felt not heard; answer = 0.6 s reverse swell into a single sub hit; sign-off = a fixed 1.0–1.5 s stinger,
  identical every episode.
- **No platform trending audio.** It aids distribution, destroys house identity, and cannot coexist with a scripted voice.

## 8. Naj, and the background casting

Voice rules are in the bible §4 and do not change. Two things change here.

**Placement.** Head above y 1600, ≤20% of frame height, no bubble or border, fades in at 3.4 s — **not in the opening frame**.
The proof is the differentiator; the avatar is the commodity.

**The background is cast per episode to the viewer's home country.** Kendall, 21 Sep:

> "If the person is from UK let them be in an office when you can see Big Ben. If they are from Singapore, let the background have
> the view of something that a Singaporean would connect to. If they in Paris let them be at a coffee shop where you can see the

## 9. Publishing

The two platforms have **diverged**. Do not run one strategy across both.

| | Instagram Reels | TikTok |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 45 s cut | 45 s cut; test the 75 s on TikTok only |
| Hashtags | **Use none, or one.** Posts with ≥1 hashtag got **31.7% fewer views** and 33.9% fewer interactions across 24.4 M posts; Instagram capped posts at five in late 2025 and now indexes captions by keyword **[STRONG]** | Hashtags still work — TikTok-side reporting has hashtag traffic up 114% YoY. Naj's brief's 12–15 across three rotating pools applies **here only** |
| Caption | Hook → one-line answer → soft authority → CTA (Naj's brief §6). Keyword-rich, because Instagram now searches captions | Same structure, plus the hashtag set |
| Disclosure | AI label on the post; profile label on (§0.1) | Self-disclose AIGC on upload |

**The CTA, from the strongest finding in the research** — Metricool, 24,364,803 posts, 375,118 accounts, Jun 2026 **[STRONG]**:

| Caption CTA | Effect |
|---|---|
| Asking for **comments** | **+203% comments** |
| Asking for **saves** | **+92% saves** |
| Posting a **question** in the caption | **+37% comments** |
| Asking for **likes** | **−4.9%** — never do this |

So the caption ends with a **keyword-triggered comment CTA and a question**: *"Comment REEM and I'll send you the floor-by-floor
list. Which building do you want me to check next?"* That rides the +203%, generates the volume, and gives a DM trigger.

**Reply to every comment in the first hours.** Posts where the creator replied earned **+21% engagement** on Instagram across
52 M posts, and the effect held on every platform studied **[STRONG]**. This is a staffing commitment, not an editing one.

**Link in bio is safe** — Mosseri, on the record, says it does not affect reach either way. But use **our own domain**, not a
shared link-in-bio service: those domains accumulate spam reports across millions of accounts and get blocked wholesale.

**Export discipline.** A clean, unwatermarked, full-bleed 1080×1920 master **per platform**. Never re-download the other
platform's copy. Instagram names low-resolution, watermarked, muted, bordered and majority-text reels as things it makes less
visible, and since 30 Apr 2026 accounts that mainly repost others' content are not recommended to non-followers at all. Four of
those five are export-settings failures. **[STRONG]**

**Cadence:** consistent but not machine-gun — high-volume identical-template posting is part of the AI-spam signature (§0.2).

### ⚠️ The editorial line on episode 2

The absent escrow tick is the strongest beat on the slate and the most dangerous. **Show the list, teach the check, and never
name the project without a tick or imply wrongdoing.** A missing tick in our data is not proof that a developer has no escrow
account, and a named accusation about a real company is a legal problem. The safe line is *"this is the tick I check for every
client"*, over the list, with the camera not resting on any one row.
