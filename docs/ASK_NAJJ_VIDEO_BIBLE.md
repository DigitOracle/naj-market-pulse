## 1. What the series is

> "A recurring short-form series where a real client question is answered by Najj's avatar **while the app does the proving on
> screen** — so every episode simultaneously (a) answers a question buyers are actually asking, and (b) demonstrates how fast and
> sharp Najj is as a broker." — Naj's brief §1

Two goals, both in every episode: **the efficiency of the realtor**, and **a real answer to a real question**.

The test for a finished cut: *could another broker in Dubai make this video?* If yes, it is not the series. The app's twin, the
registers and the plates are what nobody else has — the video exists to put them on screen while Naj answers.

**Audience:** potential buyers and relocators. Naj flags in her brief that if the target is ever *other agents* instead, the hook
and CTAs need re-tilting — **flag before building**, do not quietly re-aim.

## 2. Name and the fixed pattern

Naj's recommendation is **"Ask Najj"**, because it is the only candidate that also *generates its own input*: people comment their
questions, and those comments feed the question bank. Her alternatives were "The 60-Second Broker", "Dubai, Decoded" and
"Before You Move".

Anticipation comes from **promise + pattern**: a relatable question the viewer sees themselves in, then a fast, visual, credible
answer with the app as proof. The pattern must not drift between episodes.

⚠️ **Name collision, now forced by the opener (§9.2).** Every film opens *"Welcome to **Najma**"* and currently ends
*"Welcome to **Azimuth**"*. A viewer is welcomed to two brands in the same fifty-five seconds. **Recommendation: drop
the second welcome** — the sign-off becomes *"Dubai, decoded — one tap at a time. Complexity into clarity."* One word
out, collision gone, both lines keep their character. Still open beneath it: the files are named `NAJMA_DEMO…`, the
broker is "Naj" in our docs and "Najj" in her brief. One spelling — Kendall's call.

## 3. The shape of an episode

Naj's brief sets a **two-part shape**:

> **Part A — Hook + Question (3–6s).** A persona (image or short clip of a potential client) asks the question in first person.
> *"I'd love to move to Dubai, but I need to know where people of my nationality live."*
>
> **Part B — The Answer (20–40s).** Najj's avatar answers, with the app screen-recorded in the background executing the exact
> action that answers it. — Naj's brief §3

✅ **SETTLED 22 Sep: the two-part shape wins, and the client is a separate shot.** Kendall: *"now I need the opening
question video."* The client asks to camera in her own 10-second HeyGen Cinematic shot, generated with **no avatar
reference** so the face differs every episode — it reads as a question, not a recurring character. Naj never shares
the frame with her; the two are separate films, hard-cut.

**The question has a fixed three-part shape and only the words inside it change:**

> *"I keep walking past **[TOWER]**. Is there anything left in it? And what's **[DISTRICT]** actually like to live in?"*

**Two questions, deliberately.** The app answers the first, the dossier answers the second — which is what turns the
close (§8.1) into the second half of the reply rather than a flourish.

**Write proper nouns phonetically.** "Marina Pinnacle" came back as *"Marina Pinedic"*; "Al Habtoor" needs the same
treatment. And **state the dialogue three times** — before everything, inside the speaking beat, and in the DO NOT —
because Cinematic mode invents its own lines otherwise (§9.2).

This supersedes the 18 Sep one-voice rule *for the question only*. Inside the episode, Naj is still the only one on
screen and the only one speaking.

Both work. They are different products:

| | **One voice** (ours, 18 Sep) | **Two-part** (Naj's brief, 21 Sep) |
|---|---|---|
| Who asks | Naj reports it: *"A client asked me…"* | A persona says it to camera |
| Length | 55–90 s | 25–45 s |
| Feels like | A broker briefing you | A social format |
| Cost per episode | One avatar render | A persona clip **and** an avatar render |
| Risk | Slower; less thumb-stopping | A second face on screen dilutes Naj, and a fake client is a fake client |

**Until Kendall says otherwise, keep the one-voice rule** — it is the more recent instruction *to us* and the four filed videos
match it. The moment he says two-part, §4 and §5 change but nothing else does.

**Length is the second thing to settle.** Naj asks for 25–40 spoken words in Part B. Our filed cuts run three to four times that:

| video | cut | spoken words |
|---|---|---|
| 01 Business Bay | 1:18 | ~200 |
| 02 DAMAC Hills (Loreto) | 1:32 | ~200 |
| 03 What's hot | 1:08 | 155 |
| 04 The building page | 1:16 | 204 |
| 05 The view | 0:57 (draft) | — |

A 40-word answer is a different film: one tap, one number, out. Both are worth having — a short cut for the feed and a long cut
for the site — but they cannot be the same edit, and the app beats have to be chosen for the length before filming starts.

## 4. Naj's voice

These hold whichever shape wins, and they are the most expensive rules in this document — each came from Kendall rejecting a cut.

- **First person, Naj's own account.** *"A client came to me and said, Naj, Dubai is such a big place — help me navigate it. And it
  is a big place…"* She reports what the client asked and answers it from the app.
- **Not a narrator** ("So the client asks…", videos 01–02), **not the avatar playing the client**, **not a two-hander.** Kendall
  corrected all three on 18 Sep.
- **Few figures spoken.** The screens carry the numbers. Kendall on video 03: *"too many figures."* Two or three spoken numbers in
  a minute is plenty; the rest are read off the screen by the viewer.
- **Numbers spelled out**, "AED" written as **"dirhams"**, so the voice reads words rather than digits.
- **One paragraph**, no stage directions, no headings — HeyGen reads whatever is pasted.
- **Sign-off, unchanged since 18 Sep:** *"Welcome to Azimuth. Dubai, decoded — one tap at a time. Complexity into clarity."*
  (Najma demos only. Other DigitAlchemy HeyGen scripts keep their own.)
- **Soft authority line** for captions, from Naj's brief §6: *"14 years across Dubai's market — this is what I check for every
  client."* Spoken only if it earns its seconds.
- **Never re-cut the picture to fit the words.** Kendall, 18 Sep: *"do not cut the video, just fix the narrative."* If a beat
  drifts, move words between neighbouring beats.

## 5. Pacing — measure the voice, then write to it

**Do not assume a words-per-second rate. Measure the voice you are actually using.**

Videos 01–03 were written at **0.42 s a word**. On 20 Sep the HeyGen voice for video 04 read 154 words in **0:56** against 1:10 of
picture — **0.364 s a word** — and Naj ran eight seconds ahead of the screen by the About card. Kendall: *"timing is off."*

The method:

1. Cut the picture first. Read `data/demo/raw/marks.json` for the real second each beat starts.
2. Budget each beat: `words_allowed = beat_seconds / rate`, at the **measured** rate for that voice.
3. Write to the budget, beat by beat, and put the table in the narrative doc so the next person can see the arithmetic.
4. **Tell Kendall the duration HeyGen should show** once the script is pasted (it should match the cut, ±3 s). If it comes up
   short, the voice is faster again: nudge the voice speed down a step and re-measure — never trim the video.

## 6. The pipeline

Naj's brief §4 lays out five stages. Four of them already exist in this repo; one does not. Her brief assumes the screen recording
is the manual bottleneck — **it is not, and that is the thing to tell her.**

> "**Known manual bottleneck:** the app screen recording (Stage 3) usually can't be fully automated unless the app can be
> scripted." — Naj's brief §4

The app **is** scripted. `scripts/demo_capture.py` drives it with Playwright and records a 1080×1920 take with a drawn cursor,
timed marks and a reproducible edit. Five videos have been shot this way with no hand-recording and no hand-editing.

| Stage (Naj's brief) | Where it lives | State |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Question bank | `questions/bank.json` — 150 questions, 73 answered, tagged by topic (building_unit 18, market 16, location 15, developer 14, prices_rents 13, transport 12 …) | **built** |
| 2 · Script + copy | This bible §4–5 + the per-video narrative docs. Captions/hashtags/CTA **not yet produced** | **half** |
| 3 · Assets | `scripts/demo_capture.py` (screen), HeyGen (avatar), persona clip if the two-part shape wins | **built, minus persona** |
| 4 · Auto-edit | `cut()` in the same script — ffmpeg concat, span speed-ups, freeze close. Captions burned in with `faster_whisper` word timings | **built** |
| 5 · Publish | Not started. No scheduling, no Reels/TikTok push | **not built** |

The gap worth closing next is **Stage 2's copy** (captions, hashtags, CTA per episode — §10) and **Stage 5**.

## 7. Filming — the capture harness

`python scripts/demo_capture.py {probe|capture|cut|all}`, with `NAJMA_VIDEO` choosing the journey.

| `NAJMA_VIDEO` | journey | video |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `journey` | 01 Business Bay |
| 2 | `journey2` | 02 DAMAC Hills |
| 3 | `journey3` | superseded compare/versus concept |
| 4 | `journey4` | 03 What's hot |
| 5 | `journey5` | 04 The building page |
| 6 | `journey6` | 05 The view (draft) |
| 7 | `journey7` | 06 Aykon City — **the first cut built from numbered shots** |
| 8 | `journey8` | 07 Al Habtoor Tower — **17 shots, two pages, and the dossier scrolled** |

**Journey 7 is the reference implementation of the template**, and the one to copy. It films
`/building/businessbay/73` in the app's **native wide layout** (at 1080 px the page shows About on the left and the
filters and rings on the right, with the tower between — no injected CSS needed), declares 11 shots through the
`shot(mark, n, target, label, mode)` context manager, and closes on the building dossier. `mode="speed"` lets the cut
speed a camera move to fit its target; `mode="hold"` trims to the settled tail instead and never speeds it, which is
what a panel needs — a sped-up scroll is unreadable and a sped-up settle looks like a glitch.

**Adding an episode:** add a config branch beside the others, write `journeyN(pg, mark)`, register it in the dispatch dict in
`capture()`, and add the output filename to the dict in `cut()`. Keep the beats in the order the narrative will speak them.

### The rules that make a take usable

- **Mark every beat.** `mark("beat3")` writes `data/demo/raw/marks.json`; the cut and the whole of §5 are built on it.
- **Span names are instructions to the cut**, not labels:
  - `orbitN_start/_end` — a hand-dragged 3D turn. Recorded in slow motion (the canvas redraws on every mouse move) and played
    back at `ORBIT_TARGET` = 11 s.
  - `orbitzoomN_*` — a camera push or pull, sped to `ZOOM_TARGET` = 4 s, **but only if it recorded longer than twice the target**.
  - `orbitskipN_*` — dead time (a tap the 3D takes 5–8 s to answer, a page load, photoreal tiles sharpening). **Dropped entirely**:
    the film hard-cuts across it. Put the cursor move *inside* the skip span so the hand does not teleport.
- **The drawn cursor** (`CURSOR_JS`, element `#__cur`) must be re-injected after every navigation — a new document loses it. Hide it
  before the final held frame.
- **`glide_click`** for ordinary pages (travel, then Playwright's own click, which re-resolves position at click time).
  **`jump_click`** on heavy 3D pages — gliding there waits on redraws and costs 30 s a click.
- **Drag a dual slider's floor thumb first** (`#hlo` before `#hhi`): the high thumb sits on top of the low one. Read the value back
  and finish with arrow keys.
- **GPU flags are required for three.js pages.** `--use-angle=d3d11 --ignore-gpu-blocklist --enable-gpu`. Without them a single
  screenshot of the building page took 28 s and nothing records in real time.

### Filming a phone layout sharply (20 Sep, video 04–05)

Most of the app's best screens have a phone layout. To film it at full sharpness:

```
--force-device-scale-factor=2 --window-size=540,960   +   new_context(no_viewport=True)
```

A **real** window scale of 2 records as a sharp 1080×1920. Playwright's *emulated* `device_scale_factor` does **not** work — its
recorder captures CSS pixels, so the page lands 540 px wide in the corner of the frame. Mouse coordinates stay in CSS pixels
(540×960) while the film is 1080×1920.

Where a page has no phone layout, inject its own `max-width` rules as a style tag and say so in the storyboard — film-only CSS is
allowed, but it must be declared, and it is usually worth handing back to the app as a real fix.

### Keys and safety

- **`READ_KEY` is the owner key and never goes on camera**, never into a command line, never into a log. `refuse_owner_key()` exists
  for this.
- Film on **`CLIENT_KEY`**, the client tier. `RESIDENTS_KEY` opens the residents page.
- Keys come from `HKCU\Environment` via `winreg`. `.env` files with secrets are not read (CLAUDE.md).
- **Mask every href and URL before printing it.** A nav-href dump once echoed the client key into a transcript.

## 8. The cut

`cut()` trims the head to `marks["open"] - 0.6`, applies the span rules above, concatenates with ffmpeg at **30 fps**, and closes
with `freeze_close()` — the last frame held (5 s on the recent videos) so the sign-off lands on a still rather than on black.

**Check frames before sending anything.** A contact sheet at one frame every 3–4 seconds catches the things that ruin a take:
a panel that never opened, a card behind a scrim, a still that runs too long, a label collision. `ffmpeg -vf "fps=1/3,tile=10x2"`.

**Motion check:** measure mean frame difference per second and confirm there is no accidental still longer than about three
seconds after the opening beat. Kendall, 18 Sep: *"sitting there for a long time, almost five seconds as a still."*

## 8.1 The close — the dossier, SCROLLED

Kendall, 22 Sep: *"she should be scrolling through that four page dossier slowly and kind of explaining everything
there as well. She doesn't want to like fly through it."*

**The close is no longer `close_clip()`.** Rendering the PDF page by page and holding each one reads as a slideshow,
and this is the document the client keeps — it should feel like being walked through it, not shown it.

So the dossier's **own HTML** is opened in the browser and scrolled, easing to each heading the way a thumb does, with
a pause long enough for Naj to name what she is looking at. `_dossier_scroll()` in the harness; `DOSSIER_STOPS` is the
list of sections and the only thing that changes per episode.

**Six stops, ~3.5 s each, about 21 seconds.** For episode 07 they were: the stack · what it sells for · **who designed
and built it** · around it and the shops · who lives here · the floor plate.

**The dossier is dressed for the film.** It is authored for paper — dark type on white — and dropped into a
champagne-graded film it flares, with type too small to read on a phone. `DOSSIER_CSS` puts it on the app's own
near-black with cream type, gold headings and 2.4× zoom. **Nothing is re-worded or hidden; only the paper changes,
and it is our document.**

**Set `SHEET_PDF = None` for any episode that scrolls**, or the dossier appears twice — once scrolled, once flipped.

**It costs length.** Twenty-one seconds of dossier took episode 07 to 72 s, and with the opener and the question the
published film is about 92 s — past the Reels band. That is a real trade and it was made deliberately. A shorter
Instagram cut from the same master, with three stops instead of six, is the answer.

## 9. Avatar backgrounds — match the client's home

Kendall, WhatsApp, 21 Sep, answering *"For the Becky, any background?"*:

> "We can keep changing it up. Maybe if the person is from UK let them be in an office when you can see Big Ben. If they are from
> Singapore, let the background have the view of something that a Singaporean would connect to. If they in Paris let them be at a
> coffee shop where you can see the Eiffel Tower — so that client who is speaking can connect with the potential client who's
> coming from that country."

**The rule:** the background is cast per episode, from the nationality the episode is aimed at. It is not decoration; it is the
first half-second of "this is for you".

| Episode aimed at | Background |
|---|---|
| United Kingdom | an office with Big Ben in the window |
| Singapore | a view a Singaporean recognises |
| France | a café with the Eiffel Tower behind |
| Any | a landmark that country reads instantly, seen *through* a window or from a table — a place, not a postcard |

Two constraints we have to hold while doing it:

- **Grade.** Videos 01–03 are cut to a champagne grade with the avatar in the bottom third and the right-hand data panel kept
  clear. A bright London window must not fight the panel that carries the proof.
- **Nationality is context, never a recommendation.** The residents page says on its own face that it is *"for market
  understanding and planning content; not for recommending homes"* (methodology §11, Kendall's decision of 17 Sep). A British
  background is fine. "Move here because the British live here" is not, and video 02 is deliberately ordered so that schools,
  shops and budget choose the community and the people question is asked about a community already on the table.

## 9.2 The opener — fixed, and on every single video

**Kendall, 22 Sep: "this gets folded into the bible, every single one opens with this."**

Every Najma video, without exception, opens with the same ten-second branded shot. It is generated once and reused
unchanged. It is never re-cut, never "freshened", and never made bespoke for an episode — an opener earns its seconds
through recognition, and variety destroys the only thing it does.

**The master:** `assets/brand/najma_opener_9x16.mp4` — **approved final by Kendall, 22 Sep 2026.** Do not regenerate.

### What happens in it

A monumental gold sculpture of the Najma **N** stands alone in a bare deep-green room. Naj enters from frame left with
a model's runway walk, crosses to the sculpture and leans against it, settling her weight. As she settles, a cluster of
slender gold **towers rises out of the letterform** — and the towers **give birth to the star**, which lifts from the
tallest tower's tip and comes to rest glowing above them. She looks to camera and says:

> **"Welcome to Najma. Provenance for property."**

The last one and a half seconds are completely static. **The cut into shot 1 is hard — no dissolve.** Shot 1 opens on a
*moving* camera, so stillness into motion is the join.

### How it was made, for when it must be remade

**HeyGen Cinematic**, 9:16, 10 s, **Enhance prompt OFF**, three reference slots:

| Slot | Image | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the N alone, no towers, no star | the **start** state |
| 2 | the N with towers and star | the **end** state |
| 3 | Naj's avatar reference | the likeness |

The full shot description and the two ChatGPT prompts that generated the reference images are in
**`docs/NAJMA_OPENER_HEYGEN_CINEMATIC.md`**. The text is composited **after** generation, never generated: the wordmark
and tagline are laid on in the edit, where the type is perfect and identical every time.

### Two failures it cost to find, so nobody repeats them

1. **Cinematic mode writes its own dialogue.** The first render had her say *"And that's why staying natural and
   confident is key"* — invented entirely, because the prompt never stated the words. The line must appear **three
   times** in the description: as a block before anything else, inside the speaking beat, and in the DO NOT. Every
   other beat must be marked silent.
2. **It defaults to 720p.** This asset plays before every film. Set 1080p.

### What it does to length

```
[ OPENER 10 s ]  →  [ EPISODE 45 s ]  →  post
```

**Published length is therefore about 55 seconds**, which sits inside the 45–60 s Reels optimum rather than outside it.
The 45-second episode itself is unchanged: the opener is concatenated at assembly and is **not** in
`scripts/demo_capture.py`, which films the app and nothing else.

### Disclosure

The opener is the **first exposure**, which is where the EU AI Act requires disclosure. If the opener is ever used
alone — as a profile video, a story, a bumper — **it must carry the disclosure line itself**, because then it is the
whole film.

## 10. Caption, hashtags, CTA — the missing half of Stage 2

Not produced for videos 01–05. Required from the next episode on. From Naj's brief §6:

**Caption structure:** `[question restated as a hook]` → `[one-line answer/value]` → `[soft authority]` → `[CTA]`

**Hashtags:** 12–15, from three rotating pools — Dubai relocation, Dubai real estate/investment, and a swappable niche set
(nationality or area).

**CTAs, rotated:**
- "Comment your nationality and I'll tell you where your community is."
- "DM me 'MOVE' for a personalized area shortlist."
- "Save this for your Dubai move."
- "Book a 15-min call — link in bio."

**Hook overlay:** series title plus this episode's teaser, as a fixed graphic position across every episode.

Naj's brief §5 gives the per-question prompt template that should generate all of this in one call, from

CAPTION, HASHTAGS, CTA, HOOK_OVERLAY. **Build it against the real question bank, not a fresh list** — `questions/bank.json` already
carries the question, its topic, who asks it, where in the app it is answered, and its source.

## 11. Evidence — every claim, on screen or sourced

The discipline that makes the series defensible, and it is not optional.

- **Every spoken claim is visible on screen at the moment it is spoken**, or spoken with its source named aloud (e.g. the 2040 plan
  line in video 03).
- Each narrative doc carries a **claim → source table**. If a claim cannot be traced to a screen or a named register, it is cut.
- **Name the register, not "the data".** The Land Department units register, the Municipality floor register, the DEWA customer
  register, KHDA, RTA.
- **Say what is not known.** The register publishes counts by type, not unit numbers. Where the walls run between homes is not
  published. An indicative floor plate says "indicative" on its face.
- Nothing is mocked up. Every figure is read live from the app on the client tier.

## 14. Known limits — check before promising a beat

### 14.0 Check the facade before you film — every time

Kendall, 21 Sep, on a cut of a building with no facade: *"this looks absolutely horrible. I wouldn't put this in front of
any client."* He was right and it should never have been sent.

Two separate defects produce a blank building, and they look identical on screen:

1. **The selection repaint.** Deep-linking `?b=<id>` selects the building and repaints it by unit type, which destroys
   the facade. Fixed by the twin session in v215 — **but check the deployed version before blaming the model.** If the
   facade still vanishes on selection, you are filming an old build.
2. **The class it was given.** Measured across all 43 districts and 66,715 buildings, 83.9% of facade classes came from
   `default:low` — a guess from height. `scripts/facade_reclass.py` re-classed 607 tall buildings from the registers'
   use and completion year on 22 Sep (glass 28% → 53% of the tall stock), **but that only reaches the screen after the
   district is re-massed in CityEngine.** A repack does not carry it: the class is baked at generate time.

**The rule:** before committing a building to an episode, look at it in the twin at the angle you intend to film, on the
live build. If it is flat, pick another building or get the district re-massed first. Do not shoot around it.

### 14.1 Confident name matching is how wrong data reaches a buyer

**Four separate cases in one day, 22 Sep, all live or nearly live.** This is the most dangerous class of defect the app
has, because the wrong answer looks exactly like the right one and nothing on screen signals doubt.

| What happened | How |
|---|---|
| Four Dubai buildings served **Umm Al Quwain** floor plans | "Bayside", "Delphine", "Pristine", "Aquamarine" are Sobha Siniya Island towers; the names read as Dubai buildings |
| Five Dubai buildings served **another developer's** plans | Emaar's Creek Horizon carrying Sobha's *The Horizon*; Emaar's Grande carrying Sobha's *Creek Vistas Grande* |
| A plan bind crossed brands | Emaar's *Boulevard Heights* matched **Samana** Boulevard Heights — caught before it shipped |
| An **all-id** chain landed 5 of 25 projects on the wrong building | Binghatti Haven resolved to *The Community-Sports Arena*. The ids were faithful; the footprint binding underneath them was not |

Three things follow, and the third is the one that is easy to get wrong:

- **The harvest is not Dubai-only.** Developers sell across the UAE and their sites do not partition by emirate. 1,254
  plans over 22 projects are in Umm Al Quwain, Abu Dhabi or RAK. Every project now carries an `emirate` field.
- **Some names cannot be matched by substring at all.** Emaar has registered projects called **"May"** and **"JUNE"**;
  Sobha has **"Waves"**, which sits inside six other register projects. 29 projects carry `exact_only: true`.
- **An id chain is not self-validating.** "Use the id, not the name" was the lesson of the week and it is *incomplete*:
  ids inherit the errors of whatever binding produced them. The building's own registered name is the only independent
  check available, so a bind publishes only when the ids agree **and** the names do not contradict.

**For a video:** if an episode shows floor plans, or any figure attributed to a named building, confirm the building on
screen is the building the data came from. `data/board/plans_bind.json` carries a `building` block that has passed both
checks, and a `refused_contradicted` list that has not. Never film from the second.

### 14.3 The two-surface check — before a subject is named, not after

**Panel AND page, for any building either we or another session recommends.** Standing rule from 22 Sep.

It exists because the audit session vetted Marina Pinnacle on the map panel and the unit-mix record, everything they
said was correct, and the *building page* was serving another developer's floor plans at the same moment. They had
verified the surface they were working on and let it stand as clearance for a different one. That is not carelessness;
it is the natural cost of deep work in one lane, and it will happen again unless the check is explicit.

**And check the MODEL before committing to a subject.** Sales rank tells you nothing about whether a building will
look like anything. Measured across Business Bay's top sellers on 22 Sep:

    Peninsula Four, The Plaza   1,377 sales   56 floors   model height 16 m   <- a stub
    The EDGE                    1,286 sales   47 floors   model height 16 m   <- a stub
    Bayz 101 By Danube          1,218 sales   98 floors   model height 38 m   <- a stub
    Al Habtoor Tower            1,296 sales   93 floors   model height 345 m  <- correct

Al Habtoor was the **only** hot building whose model was right. Read `data/ce/<slug>/facade_v2.json` and compare `h`
against the floor count before a building is named on camera.

**Two surfaces can disagree because they were deployed at different times, not because either is wrong.** As of
24 Sep 2026 the live map's `amenities` layer carries the aligned DHA position guard (longitude identical and within
600 m) while the stacks' `district_amenities` still carry the old distance-only one — the new district cuts are built
on disk but only reach the site via a 32-district `build_scheme_links` run plus 32 stack pushes, and the DDA session
deliberately deferred that rather than collide with the bldgfacts and geo work landed since the last stack push, to
move about ten facilities.

So **the map and the building page's LOCATION axis can disagree on up to ~10 health facilities, each by at most
631 m**, until the next deliberate stack rebuild. That is not a regression and does not block filming: it is a
handful of clinics a few hundred metres apart on two layers, and no episode has ever put both counts in one frame.
**But do not put them in one frame** — if a shot would show the map's amenity layer and the page's LOCATION axis
together, check the two agree on that district first, or pick a different beat. A viewer cannot tell a deployment lag
from an error, and on camera it reads as the app contradicting itself.

### 14.4 Present and wired is not works

**Five defects on one page in one day, every one caught by someone about to USE the output, none by any test:**

| | how it hid |
|---|---|
| Ten buildings served another developer's floor plans | a containment match on a common word; the markup was perfect |
| `ABOUT THE BUILDING` dead since v220 on every building with a dossier | `K is not defined`, thrown on **click**. The module ran to completion on load, so a load test passed |
| DEWA occupancy shipped 22 Sep and never rendered | it lives *inside* that dead handler |
| The pillars radar drew as a black triangle | a stylesheet shipped as literal characters — `' + PILLAR_CSS + '` concatenated inside a template literal |
| 304 buildings read "sold out" when they were not | `Math.max(0, units − sales)` against resale history |

**A test can prove markup is present. It cannot prove styling arrived, a handler runs, or a number means what it says.**

So before filming a page: **open it in a browser and click the thing.** Two of those five were found by clicking a
control and reading what came back; one by asking whose a number was; one by looking at a frame. None needed code
access, and none of them would have failed a build.

**Corollary, and it is the sharper half:** three of the five were reported as fixed by someone reading the source or
the branch, while the served page still had the fault. **Check the version you are actually looking at**, and prefer
"I clicked it" over "it is wired".

### 14.2 The rest

- **The real-view page is flat in Dubai** (21 Sep). `/view` renders Google photoreal tiles; four towers tested, one given 50 s to
  load, and no 3D buildings appear — so a card saying "this side sees the Burj Khalifa" sits over a picture with no Burj in it.
  **Do not build a beat on it** until it is fixed. Reported in the video 05 draft, not yet raised with the app sessions.
- **The four-side views are hard to reach** on a building that has a floor layout: they hide behind the panel's mode switch, and in
  the desktop layout we could not reach them at all.
- **Taps on the 3D page lag 5–8 s.** Always a skip span.
- **Register vs footprint mismatches.** 127 Business Bay floors and 135 DAMAC Hills floors carry more homes than their footprint
  can hold (one permit covering several blocks). Those plates are not drawn, and a video must not claim them.
- **Off-plan buildings are thin in the units register.** Binghatti Aquarise is 27% covered, so it gets no unit numbers; Al Habtoor
  Tower is 100% and does. Pick the building for the beat you want.
- **Sold-per-floor cannot be answered.** The DLD transactions register carries no unit or floor key — only building names. "What's
  left" stays at building grain. (Confirmed by the DDA session, 20 Sep.)
- **DAMAC Hills has no 3D model** in the app yet, so nothing there can be filmed in the twin.
