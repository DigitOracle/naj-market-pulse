## Why this episode exists

Sales tell you what changed hands. Ejari tells you what was let. Permits tell you what was allowed. **A meter connected in
someone's name is the only thing in any Dubai register that means a person.** That is the whole pitch, and it is true:
2,103 buildings are published citywide, 1,284 of them named, and no portal in this market can answer the question at all.

And the answer for this building has a shape rather than a direction: **a tower that opened in April 2011 took 610 of its
769 connections in 2025**. Fourteen years standing, and most of a building's worth of homes turning over in one year.

**Said aloud, and on screen, because it is the honest half:** *a connection is not a home.* The page says it in its own
words - *"one home let three times is three connections, so this cannot be divided by the number of homes to give an
occupancy rate"* - and that sentence is held as text under the payoff number.

### The trap in this episode, written down so nobody walks into it

**769 meters connected. 772 homes.** Three apart. If those two numbers ever share a sentence or a frame, a viewer hears
*"99.6% occupied"* - which is exactly what the data does not say and cannot say. **They are deliberately separated: 772 is
spoken at 7.0 s and 769 at 27.0 s, twenty seconds apart, and they never appear on screen together.** Any re-cut must
preserve that gap.

### Not said, because nothing supports it

- Which individual homes are occupied. The register publishes connections per building, never per unit.
- An occupancy rate, in any form.
- A per-type yield. The panel shows 9.2% against all three bedroom types; that is one building-level figure repeated, not
  a yield that varies by type. If yield is mentioned at all it is *"about nine percent for the building"*.
- Anything about the 66 storeys in the massing. **The number is 72 floors** - this building is one of only 160 citywide
  whose floor count comes from a Municipality permit rather than our model.
