## The narrative, as one paragraph — paced to the screen

Written to the seconds each beat has on screen, at about 0.42 seconds a word (see the Carson
narrative for the lesson: 1:44 of speech against 1:32 of picture drifts from the first beat). Numbers
spelled out, "AED" written as "dirhams".

> So the client asks — she's British — for somewhere that feels like home. She's thinking about DAMAC
> Hills, and the map opens on it. Are there British schools? Ten within three kilometres, five British
> curriculum — Ranches Primary, rated very good. She taps it: phone, email, website, directions,
> WhatsApp, a QR code. Everything here is a link. Is there a Spinneys? Two, inside a kilometre. Two
> bedrooms, up to one point eight million dirhams, and the map narrows as she drags: thirteen homes
> here, eight hundred and seventy-nine across Dubai. Which one? The matches list out, and she picks
> Loreto — five hundred and four apartments, low-rise on the golf course, a two-bedroom at one point
> seven million. And the question underneath all the others: who actually lives here? The residents
> map shades the whole city by nationality. She chooses United Kingdom, ten percent or more, and
> twenty-three communities light up. DAMAC Hills: Europe twenty-six percent, and inside that, British
> thirteen — the largest group from Europe. And then the sheet she takes away: what Loreto sells and
> rents for, seven hundred and fifty registered sales, the furnished interiors, completed in twenty
> sixteen. Welcome to Azimuth. Dubai, decoded — one tap at a time. Complexity into clarity.
